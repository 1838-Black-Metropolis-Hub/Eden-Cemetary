STOP 1Tour stop (a section, not a single grave) Grave discussed on the walk Plan view · positions approximate
Stop 1
Richard Allen Namesake of the section, not buried here. In 1787 Allen and Absalom Jones founded the Free African Society, making care for the sick and the dead a shared community duty.
Stop 2
Lehmann Named for Martin J. Lehmann, cigar maker and treasurer of the Eden Cemetery Company — one of the five founders who secured land, records, and the cemetery’s future.
Stop 3
Lebanon Preserves the name of an 1849 Philadelphia cemetery condemned in 1899; thousands of remains came to Eden in 1903. James Forten, Octavius Catto, Caroline LeCount, William Still, Caroline Still Anderson.
Stop 4
John Brown Named for the abolitionist, who is not buried here. Frances Ellen Watkins Harper, poet and abolitionist, was buried in this section in 1911.
Uneven ground — proceed into the lots with caution. Follow the stops in order. This walk discusses racism, grave disturbance, and the movement of human remains. Eden is an active cemetery — please respect graves, services, and other visitors. Eden Cemetery · 1434 Springfield Rd, Collingdale PA 19023 · 610‑583‑8737
